## 3. The 19 candidates and what the checks found

**keep ReCall on the home screen with a distinct store name** (2).

**How it was checked (2026-09-24):**
- **US trademarks:** the USPTO's own search database (the engine behind tmsearch.uspto.gov), live marks
  only. Every candidate was run through exact, fuzzy and prefix matches; the finalists also through
  sound-alikes. The Justia mirror was used as a cross-check on the finalists.

  domains, the site itself was fetched to classify it as *in use*, *parked or for sale*, or *no site*.
- **Google Play and the web:** Google Play blocks automated search, so Play results come from a web search
  restricted to play.google.com. That is not a full Play search. **Ravi: two minutes typing each finalist
  into the Play Store app closes that gap.** Chrome on the Mac wasn't reachable to do it this session.

## 4. The board on the finalists

The four that came back clean (or nearly), plus the ReCall path so it's judged fairly (§6).

**Wherly.** *"Where" without saying it.*
- **For:** Noor: "the best-sounding word here. The h and the l stand tall, the y gives it a tail, and it
  sets well small. It says *where* the way *Spotify* says *spot*." Sam: "no live mark anywhere near it
  in the goods that matter. That's rare in this category." Maya: "findable. Type it and you get us."
  Peter: "sounds like a real app."
- **Against:** Evelyn: "How would I spell that to Nisha on the phone? W-H-E-R… or W-E-A-R?" This is
  **criterion 2, and Wherly half-fails it**: *wherely.com* and *wherely.app* are someone else's.
  Harold: "sounds like a toy." Priyanka: we'd need getwherly.com (free) or to buy wherly.com at an unknown
  price.

**Thingspot.** *The plainest one.*
- **For:** Robert: "I know what that is." Dan and Leila: it fits the garage and the locker without
  thinking. Evelyn: two words she already knows, so she can spell it on the phone. Priyanka: `.app` is free.
- **Against:** Noor: "It's a category, not a name. It will never feel like *hers*. And it's a
  password-shaped word: lots of flat letters, no rhythm." Sam: "no live marks today, but *suggestive*
  names are the weakest kind you can still register, and 'things' + 'spot' is a crowded pair (Spot It,
  PinSpot, WhereThings)." Dr Kim is neutral: nothing clinical, nothing warm.

**Wherewell.** *Know where, and all's well.*
- **For:** June: "that's lovely, like the name of a village." Margaret: "I'd say that out loud without
  minding." Noor: "the two W's make a symmetrical wordmark." `.app` and getwherewell.com are free.
- **Against:** Sam: **WEARWELL, a class 42 filing from 2026-08-21 for AI *wardrobe management***, sounds
  the same. An examiner could call that confusingly similar. Noor: "the *-well* ending reads as a
  wellness brand, the one thing the identity brief says we're not." Dan: "sounds like a spa." Priyanka:
  the .com is $16,000. Devin: 9 letters, and two wide W's, so check it under the icon.

**Hither.** *"Come here," said old-fashioned.*
- **For:** Noor: "the most beautiful word on the list." June and Margaret love it. No app, no exact mark.
- **Against:** Sam: **HITHER HIVE** is a mark for *home organization services, sorting household
  belongings*, published for opposition this year. It is the closest in meaning of any conflict we found.
  Maya: most people under 60 don't know the word, which fails "say it across the garage". Robert laughs at
  *come hither*; the Justia list shows adult-product marks built on that phrase. In Spanish the H is silent.

**Dr Kim and Harold on all four:** none of them reads as a memory aid, which is the point. Harold would
have one of them on his home screen; he wouldn't have *ReCall*.

**Unusual: nobody defended any of the four without an objection.** That's as it should be.

## 6. The ReCall path, judged fairly

*Keep "ReCall" under the icon; list the app under a longer unique store name.*

- **For (Maya):** it's what Tanya built, what Dad's phone already says, and the story everyone knows.
  Apple only requires the *store* name to be unique; the name under the icon doesn't have to be. So this
  works technically.
- **Against, trademark (Sam):** a store suffix doesn't make "ReCall" ours. RECALL is **registered** for
  software in class 9 and class 42, and filed twice more. The trademark is the word people say, not the
  store listing.
- **Against, findability (Maya concedes):** search "recall" on the App Store and we come after Recall
  (recall.it), Recall Flashcards, recall-alert apps and Microsoft.
- **Against, dignity (Dr Kim):** *delayed recall* is a test item. "It's the word from the worst morning
  of her year."
- **Against, look (Noor):** the mid-word capital C.

Risk: **high.** It stays on the table because it's the cheapest path in the short run, not because
anyone on the board recommends it.

## 7. Before we commit: an attorney's clearance search

What we did is a knock-out screen. An attorney's (or a search firm's) clearance search typically covers:
- **Federal:** US registrations *and* pending applications, including phonetic and visual variants
  (Wherly/Whirly/Wherely), in the classes we'd use (**9** downloadable app, **42** software as a service,
  and probably **45** if the support-network features grow) and in related classes.
- **State registrations** and **common-law use**: business names, domain names, app stores, social
  handles, and unregistered products in use (these carry rights in the US even without registration).
- **A written opinion** on likelihood of confusion, on whether the name is too descriptive to register,
  and on how to word the goods and services when we file.
- **International**, if we launch beyond the US (Spanish-speaking markets, India): WIPO's Global Brand
  Database, the EU (EUIPO) and India's registry, or a Madrid Protocol filing plan.

Cost varies by firm and scope; ask for a quote before ordering. **Nothing has been filed, bought or reserved.**
The domains marked "free" are free *today*; a domain is only held once someone registers it, and
that costs money, so it needs your go-ahead.

## 8. Screened out along the way (35 names)

All run through the same USPTO, App Store and domain checks (the script and trimmed raw results are in
`05_Research/naming/`; rerun it for any new name). The reason each was dropped:

- **A "find my stuff" app with the name already exists on the App Store:** Cubby ("Cubby – Home
  Inventory", "Cubby — where did I put it"), Nookly ("Nookly: Home Inventory"), Thingly ("Thingly – Home
  Inventory"), Tuckaway ("TuckAway: Storage Organizer", "Tuckaway: Where Things Live"), Stowly (a
  QR box organiser; also sounds like *slowly*), Findle ("Findle Inventory"), Rummage ("NoRummage: Box
  Organization"), Everyplace ("Everyplace — Save Your Spots").
- **Putaway:** "PutAway – Home Edition" exists, and the name contains *puta*, which is rude in Spanish.
  Criterion 7 in action.
- **Crowded with live marks in software (9/42) or big brands:** Trove (27 live marks, one for organising
  and indexing software), Loci (11, incl. a Bluetooth item-finder filing; also close to Spanish *loca*),
  Retriever (22), Ferret (14, incl. locating software), Knack (14; *naak* is "nose" in Hindi), Hutch
  (Hutch Games), Mantel, Homing (Homing, Inc.), Dibs (1stDibs and others), Keepsy, Stashly (live STASHLY
  filing), Placely (PLACELY mark), Spotly, Aqui, Voila.
- **A major brand:** Whatnot (the live-shopping marketplace).
- **Other:** Tuckbox (a card-scanner and a finance app), Stowaway (marks, packing apps), Hidey (privacy
  apps), Foundly (an app, many marks), Placemark (five apps), Hereabout (a live mark in 42), Wherely (both
  domains taken; kept only as Wherly's misspelling), Tuckle (an app), Findery (a former location-notes
  network).
- **Reserve, no conflict found:** **Thereabout** (10 letters; the board thought it too wistful). It can
  come back if the finalists fail.
