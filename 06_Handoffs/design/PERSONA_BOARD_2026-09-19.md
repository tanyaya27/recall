## MARGARET, 74 — the owner

I opened the hamburger and saw *People* second. Good; it is the row I would look for once, then never again. The empty card, *Nobody else can see your things yet.*, is the first thing in this app that told me the truth about who sees what, and it told me before I had to ask. Keep it.

The consent line — *Anyone you invite will see the photos of your things and where they are — except things you mark Only me* — I read twice and understood once. That is the right sentence. Do not add "inside your home"; I know where my things are.

*Only me* is exactly how I think about the drawer with the letters. But *Keep the scissors to yourself?* sounds like something said to a child. Say *Stop sharing the scissors?*

*Can help* is right; it is what Robert does. *Can look* is wrong. Nobody "can look" at me but a nurse. Peter can *see* my things — I do not care that *Find item* also uses the word see; that collision is yours, not mine. If you will not say *see*, do not say *look*.

*Sign in to share* — *So your things stay yours on any phone, sign in once* — fine, if the phone does it with my face. The moment it asks me for a password I have not typed in three years, I tap *Not now* and the invite is dead, and I may not come back to it. Test that path on my phone, not Peter's.

*Show who added each photo*: I would switch it on, day one. It is not surveillance of Robert; it is the difference between my memory and his, and I want to know which is which.

The status line: off is right. I will turn it on for Peter when he asks — but then I want to see, on my own screen, the same line he sees. Otherwise I am guessing what he knows.

Day one: glasses, the key bowl, Robert as *Can help*. What would stop me: the word *caregiver* anywhere.

## ROBERT, 76 — the husband, same house

I brought her the app, so I will be the first name in *People*, and *Can help* is the right size for me. I fix the names she mis-types and re-photograph the teapot when it moves. *Add photo · Edit* is what I do; Devin's *Add photo* only would have me asking her to rename things across the kitchen.

Two ReCalls cross-shared: in theory. In practice I will log everything into hers for the first month, because the kitchen is hers in the way the garage is mine, and one grid is one grid. My glasses and the hearing-aid charger can wait. When I do start mine, the thing that frightens me is *Margaret's ReCall ▾*: it reopens on whichever was last open, and I will log my glasses into her ReCall on a Tuesday and hers into mine on a Wednesday. Put whose it is on the *Log item* button itself — *Log in Margaret's ReCall* — not only in the title line I stopped reading a week in.

The kitchen scissors: they are not anyone's, they live in the drawer. If I logged them in mine by mistake, *Give the scissors to Margaret?* is the wrong verb — I am not giving her anything. *Move the scissors to Margaret's ReCall?* Keep the rest; *You will still be able to help with it* is exactly the reassurance I need.

What I cannot do as *Can help* will annoy me once a week: she logs the teapot twice and I cannot remove the duplicate. I understand why. Let me at least *Remove old photos…* — you have — and let me tell her in the app that there are two, not across the room.

Being removed: in the same house that is a conversation, not a screen. *Margaret's ReCall is no longer shared with you.* is fine. Do not add a button to argue with her.

What would stop me: sign-in asking me to create anything. I have one Google account and I do not remember which.

## PETER, 46 — the son, 800 miles away

The first minute works. Link, *Continue with Apple*, *Margaret's ReCall*: I did it on the train. *Sign in so she knows it's you* is the sentence that stops my wife asking why photos need a sign-in. Keep the explaining line.

*Can look* only: correct for me on a normal week. What I actually use is *Find item* while she is on the phone saying "the blue folder". A card with a photo and a place is enough.

The status line off by default: it is why I installed the app, and I accept off — provided the suggestion *Peter can look but not change. Want him to see when you last used the app?* really does appear straight after she sends my link, because I will be on FaceTime saying "tap Yes". *Last opened Tuesday* on a Friday is the line I need and it is not an alarm. Do not make it one.

Setting up from here is the hole. The board says she is anonymous until the first guest, so I cannot invite myself; every tap of *People → Invite someone… → Can look → Sign in to share → Continue with Apple → Send a link… → Messages → Peter* happens on her phone with me narrating over video. That is eight taps, two of them sign-ins, and one wrong turn loses the invite. I need either a remote-setup path — I install, I start the invite, she confirms with one tap — or the flow rehearsed until it survives a 74-year-old and a laggy FaceTime.

Also missing: I pay. The AI key is billed to the owner, and she will not enter a card. Billing has to sit on my account while the ReCall stays hers.

On a bad week I need her to move me to *Can help* — one tap on her side — so I can log the things she texts me photos of. And I need to know she cannot lock herself out: if she gets a new phone, Apple sign-in must bring everything back without me.

## LINDA, 70 — the friend, prospective customer

I have the same thing Margaret has, and my daughter is in Lisbon. I have been saying no to this app for a month.

*Nobody else can see your things yet.* is the line that got me. Nobody, unless I say. I want that to be true forever if I want it to: the app must be complete with no one invited. Nothing on the board says otherwise, and I need it said, once, in the empty state: *You never have to invite anyone.*

The consent line is honest, and honesty is reassuring even when the content is not. *Anyone you invite will see the photos of your things and where they are.* That is the deal, and it is stated before I make it. Good.

*Let my people see when I last used the app* frightened me. That is how it starts: first they see when I opened it, then they see that I didn't, then someone rings. I know it is off. I know I choose. It still reads like a door I would be asked to open. Put it below everything else, and say what they will *not* see.

*Only me* I liked. *Remove* with *Nothing is deleted* I liked. *Sign in to share — so your things stay yours on any phone* made me ask: whose phones? Say *on a new phone*.

What I would say to Margaret: start with nobody, then share with me. Not Robert, not Peter — me. We both lose things, we are two floors apart, and if she can *see* my things and I hers, we can find each other's reading glasses over the phone without either of us being anybody's carer. That is the version of this I would pay for, and it is not on the board — two people, both *Can help* in each other's ReCall, both owners, neither looked after.

## DR KIM — geriatric neuropsychologist

This model respects autonomy in the ways that count: one owner, a visible list, revocable at will, roles she chose in her own words, *Nothing is deleted* on removal, and a removal screen that tells Peter *she chose* with no button to appeal. Editors cannot delete or reshare. The status line is opt-in and stays a line. *Owner* never appears because it does not need to. This is a good foundation.

Where a helper still drifts into proxy:

1. **Authorship.** If Robert logs most things, the record becomes Robert's account of her home. *Show who added each photo* off by default hides that drift from the one person who should see it. Default it on, or show in People *Robert has added 14 things this week*. Margaret says she would turn it on; the ones who would not are the ones at risk.
2. **Setup consent.** Peter narrating eight taps over FaceTime produces a tapped consent, not a read one. No preselected role (agree with Devin), the consent line above the button, and no remote path that lets a helper send the invite on her behalf.
3. **Role escalation on a bad week.** *Can look → Can help* is one tap on a bad day and stays forever. Show *since Sep 12* on the row so she sees roles as decisions with dates, not fixtures.
4. **Inversion.** Robert logging her medication in his ReCall and sharing it to her puts her routine under his ownership. The title-on-the-button Robert asked for guards this.

The app must never: show a helper anything she cannot see herself; hold a role she cannot change; infer decline from usage or colour the status line; call anyone a caregiver or patient; let a helper make a thing private or delete it; gate her own use behind a sign-in; or let Transfer of the whole ReCall happen from anyone's phone but hers, awake, confirmed.

Add: an owner-side *See what Peter sees* preview. Margaret asked for it and it is the cheapest dignity feature on the board.

## AGREEMENTS

- Margaret owns; *People* is her list; the empty state and the consent sentence are right and should be said exactly twice.
- The status line is off by default, is a line and never an alarm, and she should be able to see what her people see.
- Editors cannot remove things or change who sees them; *Nothing is deleted* on removal; no "ask her" button when someone is removed.
- Peter's three-tap first minute works; keep *Sign in so she knows it's you*.
- *Sign in to share* is the screen that can lose her; it must work with face/passkey and never a typed password.
- The invite sheet has no preselected role.
- Whose ReCall a new thing goes into must be visible at the moment of logging, not only in the title line.
- No *caregiver*, *patient* or *owner* on any screen.

## DISAGREEMENTS

1. **Can look vs Can see.** Margaret and Linda: *look* sounds like being watched; say *see*, collision with *Find item* be damned. Peter and Dr Kim: *look* is fine and the explaining line does the work. Robert: no view.
2. **Show who added each photo — default.** Dr Kim and Robert: on. Margaret: off as proposed, she would switch it on herself. Peter: neutral. Linda: off, fewer names on her screen.
3. **Editor removing duplicates.** Robert: let *Can help* remove an obvious duplicate. Margaret and Dr Kim: no; a helper who can delete is a proxy. Peter: no.
4. **Remote setup.** Peter: a helper-started invite that she confirms with one tap. Dr Kim and Margaret: every tap on her phone, by her. Linda: agrees with Dr Kim.
5. **Two ReCalls in one house.** Robert: one (hers) in practice for months; the switcher is a hazard. Peter and Dr Kim: two, with the *Log in Margaret's ReCall* button as the guard. Linda: two, symmetric, is the product she wants.
6. **Transfer copy.** Robert: *Move the scissors to Margaret's ReCall?* Margaret: *Give* is warmer and she likes it. Others: indifferent.
7. **Status-line switch placement.** Linda: last, below everything, with what they will not see. Peter: directly after a *Can look* invite so she sees it while he is on the call. Margaret: both.
8. **Billing.** Peter: must be on his account while the ReCall is hers; not on the board. Dr Kim: acceptable only if payment confers no role. Others: not their problem.
