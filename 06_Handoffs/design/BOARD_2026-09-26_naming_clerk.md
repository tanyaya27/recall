## 2. What the checks say about the word "clerk" itself

- **CLERK is registered twice in class 42** (software as a service): [97612969](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=97612969&caseType=SERIAL_NO&searchType=statusSearch)
  (Patiently LLC, electronic health records) and [90363784](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=90363784&caseType=SERIAL_NO&searchType=statusSearch)
  (Clerk Retail, retail software). 24 live marks containing CLERK sit in classes 9/42/45/35, most of them
  "X + clerk": DIGITAL CLERK, PEOPLE CLERK, CLOUD CLERK, PRO-CLERK, SKYCLERK, VCLERK, XCLERK, COCLERK.
- **Sam:** that pattern tells you how an examiner reads it. In software, *clerk* describes software that does
  clerical work, so in any "X + clerk" name the protectable part is mostly the X. *MyClerk* is the weakest shape of
  all: "my" + a descriptive word.
- **Maya:** typing "clerk" in the App Store brings up Clerk Chat, Clerk AI notes and court-clerk apps first. A
  clerk name has to be typed in full to be found.

## 4. The boards on the clerk family (the disagreements)

**Why the idea is attractive.** Harold: *"A clerk works for me. That's the first one I'd have on my phone without
being embarrassed."* Dr Kim: it's the only idea so far with dignity built in: she is the employer, not the patient.
Margaret: *"I like that it's mine and it does what I tell it."* Evelyn: for papers and appointments it's exactly
right.

**Why it's hard.**
- **Dan:** "A clerk's a desk job. It's not in my garage." (Leila disagrees: "the storage place has a clerk; that's
  who knows where your unit is.")
- **Dr Kim:** in UK and Indian hospitals, *clerking* is taking a patient's history on admission. Mild, but the
  medical sense is there for Nisha's generation of doctors.
- **Noor (sound):** *clerk* is one hard, closed syllable that stops dead on the k. And it's said three ways: *klurk*
  (US), *klark* (UK, India). Say *Clerkit* once and people may write *Clarkit* (the Evelyn test).
- **Criterion 7, Hindi (checked by Claude, not a native speaker):** क्लर्क (*klark*) is an everyday loanword, and it
  means the low-ranked government office clerk: files, queues, red tape. Not rude, but not flattering. Ask a Hindi
  speaker before picking.
- **Peter:** "Clerk sounds like business software." Every clerk result on the web is B2B (sign-in, e-commerce, legal).

**On the four finalists:**

|---|---|---|

**The out-list, briefly:** MyClerk isn't just a taken .com: myclerk.com is an AI document-management product,
myclerk.io another service, and an IETF draft uses *MyClerk* for family systems. Clerkly collides with CLERKY and
with tryclerkly.com, which does Evelyn's bills and deadlines.

**Where the room splits (not a consensus):**
- **Sam and Maya:** the clerk family ranks **below Wherly** on protectability and findability. Clerk is a crowded,
  descriptive word in software; Wherly is invented and clean.
- **Harold, Dr Kim and Margaret:** the clerk *idea* beats Wherly on meaning. Wherly says *where*; a clerk says *someone
  keeps track for me*, which is the dignity point.
- **Noor:** Clerkit is the only clerk name she'd design a wordmark for; she still prefers Wherly's sound.
- **Priyanka:** if a .com matters, Thingclerk is the only name in either round that gets one without a broker.
- **Nobody** defended MyClerk, Clerkly or Clerkwell once they'd seen the checks.
