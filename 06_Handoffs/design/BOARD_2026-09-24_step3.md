## A. Private by default (Ravi's rule, DECISIONS 2026-09-24)

**What's drawn.** When a thing looks private (passwords, PINs, bank or account papers, medical or insurance papers, ID, keys to a safe or locker), it starts as **Only me**. She is told on the same screen as the photo, with one tap to undo it:
- **One thing (A1):** a note on the photo card: *"Kept private: this looks like passwords. Only you will see it. Share it instead."*
- **Several (A2):** a yellow **Only me** chip in the strip, plus *"Share it"*. The camera stays open.
- **Write it down (A3):** the *Keep this private* switch is already on, with one line saying why.
- **A helper logging (A4):** see the disagreement below.

**How ReCall decides**
1. **The AI.** It answers "5. PRIVATE" when it names the photo, and returns `private: true` with a reason of a few words ("looks like passwords"). This costs nothing extra because it rides on the call that already happens.
2. **A word list** for anything typed or spoken: password, PIN, passcode, bank, account, SSN, social security, passport, medical, prescription, insurance, will, deed, safe, locker, tax, etc. It works with no AI at all, and it also runs on a name the AI returns.
3. **Either one is enough.** A false "private" costs her one tap. A missed one costs a secret shown to a helper. So the rule leans toward private.

**The positions**
- **Maya (PM):** ship it. The notice must say *why* in her words ("looks like passwords"). A bare lock icon would feel like the app decided something behind her back.
- **Devin (design):** wants a note, not a question: nothing to answer, and she can carry on. He argued against a pop-up. In Several the yellow chip is the whole message, and the review screen shows it again.
- **Priyanka (engineer):** keep it cheap. Add one line to the naming prompt and a word list in `lib/sensitive.js`. The switch already exists (`private`, `roles:{}`, `sharedWith:[]`). She wants "Share it instead" to write `privateAuto: false`, so ReCall never flips it back.
- **Sam (architect):** privacy is enforced by the rules, not the screen. Today only the owner can make a thing private, and he wants to keep it that way (A4 below).
- **Dr Kim (HIPAA-adjacent persona):** the word list must match *medical* words in a name, not only in photos. A written "Metformin refill slip" should start private. *Agreed.*
- **Margaret (persona):** "If Robert logs my pill organiser, I don't want the kids seeing it." That's the pull behind A4.

**Disagreement: a helper logs something private (A4)**
- **Drawn (Sam):** the helper is warned, *"Only Margaret can keep things to herself. If you save it, everyone in her ReCall sees it"*, and offered *Don't save it*. Rules unchanged.
- **Alternative (Margaret, Maya):** let a helper save it straight into Margaret's private things. It vanishes from the helper's view the moment it's saved. This needs a rule change: a helper may *create* with `private: true` but never read it afterwards. It is doable and the real rules engine can test it, but it's a new kind of write.
- **Board lean:** the drawn version now; the alternative when the support network work resumes (#11–13).

## B. Places inside places, and boxes that move (#7)

**What's the same in both options.**
- A place can have a `parent`, a field already reserved on the place doc since 09-15.
- A place can be a **container** (tin, box, bag), which moves and carries its things with it.
- Find answers outward, each level with its own photo: *Bank locker key → in the Blue tin → on the Top shelf → of the Bedroom wardrobe* (B3).
- Places becomes a tree with box badges (B4).
- A box has its own card listing its things and a **Move this box…** button (B5). Moving Box 14 moves all 13 things in one change.

**B1: Drill down (taps).** Pick *Bedroom wardrobe*, then *Top shelf*, and ReCall asks *"In something on the top shelf?"*: *Blue tin · Shoe box · + Something new… · Just on the top shelf*. Next time the usual place is already chosen and the path is shown underneath (B1 frame 2).
- For: no typing, no AI, nothing to misread, and it works offline.
- Against: 2–3 extra taps the *first* time something goes somewhere new. At Largest text, "Just on the top shelf" drops below the fold (L1).

**B2: Say it all (one line).** She types or dictates *"blue tin, top shelf of the bedroom wardrobe"* and ReCall shows how it read it as a breadcrumb: *Bedroom wardrobe › Top shelf › Blue tin*. The AI splits it; a fallback splits on *in / on / of / commas*.
- For: fastest for anyone who talks. It matches Ravi's 09-15 note (*"hierarchy… built by the system in the background, never by the user"*), and it matches Write it down, which already takes a typed place.
- Against: a wrong split has to be fixed by tapping a crumb. And "top shelf" in the wardrobe and "top shelf" in the garage must be told apart; ReCall asks only when a name already exists in two places.

**Positions**
- **Devin:** B2, with one change: tapping a crumb opens B1's drill-down at that level. That's how a split gets fixed, so both exist and B2 is the front door.
- **Priyanka:** B2 costs more, one AI prompt plus the fallback splitter and its tests. B1 is pure UI. Either way the data model and the answer chain are the same work (most of #7).
- **Robert (garage, fasteners):** B1. "I'm standing in the garage with oily hands. I'm not typing."
- **Linda (storage locker):** B2. "Box 14, back left of unit 214" is how she already labels things.
- **Sam:** it's the same tree underneath, so the choice is only about the front door and can change later without migrating data.
- **Board recommendation:** **B2 as the front door, B1 as the way to fix a crumb**, with **the usual place pre-chosen** (B1 frame 2) in both. Robert's case is mostly covered by the usual place and by Everything in view.

## D. "On this phone only" (Ravi, mid-review 09-24)

**The idea.** A step beyond *Only me*: the thing never leaves the phone. Another device signed in to the same account doesn't show it, only a line such as *"3 things are kept only on Mom's iPhone."*

**Why it's worth having.** *Only me* hides a thing from other people, but it is still stored in ReCall's cloud. Anyone who can open the Firebase project (Tanya and Ravi as its owners) could read it in the console, and so could anyone who gets into her account. For a password notebook, some people will want nothing stored in the cloud at all.

**It doesn't undo the 09-15 ruling.** DECISIONS 09-15 (round 7) settled that *Only me* means private to the *person*: her iPad, both iPhones. That stays. *On this phone only* would be a separate, stronger choice with its own name, never what *Only me* quietly means.

**The positions**
- **Sam (architect): yes, but not in the web app.** A web app's storage on iPhone is not a safe home for a record meant to last ten years. Safari can clear it when the phone runs low on space, and it isn't reliably carried over to a new phone. A native app (step 4) keeps its data inside the app, which the iPhone's own encrypted backup and phone-to-phone transfer carry over. So it belongs in step 4.
- **Priyanka (engineer):** the photo would still go to the AI for naming. For an on-phone thing, naming has to happen on the phone (Apple's on-device image labels in the native app) or be typed. In the web app today there's no way to keep the photo on the phone and still have it named.
- **Maya (PM):** losing a thing is the worst failure a memory app can have. So *"On this phone only"* must say plainly, once, that it goes if the phone is lost without a backup, and the count on other devices is the reminder that it exists.
- **Devin (design):** a third choice next to Can see / Only me, worded *"On this phone only"*. It's never chosen automatically; the private-by-default note offers it: *"Kept private… Share it instead · Keep it on this phone only."*
- **Dr Kim (persona):** the stronger version she'd actually want is *encrypted*: stored in the cloud, but only her phone holds the key, so nobody running ReCall can read it, and it survives a lost phone if she has a second device or a recovery code. More work; same native step.

**Board recommendation.** Yes, as a step 4 (native) feature, with encryption considered alongside it. Until then:
1. *Only me* wording stays honest. The settings help line says other people can't see it, not that nobody can.
2. When a *typed* name contains what looks like the secret itself (a 4–8 digit number next to "PIN", or "password:" followed by text), ReCall says *"ReCall remembers where things are. Don't type the PIN itself"* and offers to take it out. This goes in the step 3 build with the word list.
