# ReCall — identity design, starting from scratch

Paste everything below the line into Claude Design as the first message of a new design.

---

I want to design the visual identity for **ReCall**: the app icon, a wordmark, and a small mark for inside the app. Start fresh. There is no earlier brief to follow; treat this message as the brief, and ask me before assuming anything it doesn't cover.

## What ReCall is

A phone app (a web app added to the home screen today; a native iPhone app later) for a competent adult with early memory loss. She photographs where she puts things (glasses, keys, pills, the folder with the tax papers), and later asks "where are my glasses?" and gets the photo back with the place and the time. Family members she invites can see or help, but it is **her** app and **her** things. It is not a medical device, not a tracker, not a caregiver dashboard.

The person to design for is in her seventies, sharp, proud, a little embarrassed to need help, and reads her phone at the largest text size. She should be glad to have this icon on her first home screen next to Photos and Messages, and nobody glancing at her phone should read it as "she has dementia".

## What the identity should feel like

Calm, domestic, trustworthy, quietly capable. Closer to a well-made notebook or a good kitchen tool than to a health app or a tech startup. Warm, not cute. Adult, not clinical.

Ideas the name and product suggest, which you can explore or reject: a photo remembered (a print, a corner, a frame), a place (a pin, a shelf, a drawer, a table), finding (a gentle look, not a magnifying-glass cliché), a thread back to something. Explore at least three genuinely different directions before refining any of them.

## Hard constraints

1. **No medical, brain, or memory-loss imagery**: no brains, pills, crosses, heartbeats, puzzle pieces, forget-me-nots, strings tied round fingers, or anything that signals illness or care.
2. **No surveillance imagery**: no eyes, cameras-as-watching, location-tracking radars, or targets.
3. **No generic tech marks**: no gradient blobs, sparkles, AI stars, or chat bubbles.
4. **Legible at 29 × 29 px** (the smallest iOS icon size) and on a busy wallpaper; one clear shape, no fine detail, no text inside the icon.
5. **Works in the app's four palettes** and on both light and dark home screens (below).
6. **High contrast**: the key shape must pass WCAG 3:1 against its background.
7. No reference to an existing brand's logo, mascot or icon style.

The app uses the phone's own system font (San Francisco on iPhone) everywhere, on purpose. The wordmark may use a different face, but it must read cleanly beside system text.

## What I want to see, in this order

1. **Three to five distinct directions**, each as one board: the icon at large size, a one-line rationale, and the risks of that direction.
2. **A true-scale strip for each direction**: the icon at the real iOS sizes (1024, 180, 120, 87, 80, 60, 58, 40, 29 px), shown at 1:1, on Linen, on Dusk, on a photo wallpaper, and on a plain black background.
3. **An app-icon view**: the icon on a realistic iPhone home-screen grid next to generic system-style neighbours, in light mode, dark mode and the iOS tinted mode, with the label "ReCall" under it.
4. After I pick a direction: the wordmark, a one-colour version, the in-app small mark (for the top of the menu), and the splash/loading screen.

## How I'll decide

I will reject anything that looks like a health app, anything that needs explaining, and anything that loses its shape below 60 px. I'll prefer the direction she'd most want to tap. Show me the disagreements between directions rather than blending them into one.
